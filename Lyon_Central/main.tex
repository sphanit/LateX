\noindent My long-term goal is to integrate robots into everyday life as co-workers and assistants (Fig.~1). This requires motion planning that remains robust to disturbances and failures not only in controlled settings but in uncontrolled, human-populated environments, where the robot must perceive and understand the situation, communicate, react appropriately, and know when to stop. \update{Current human-aware navigation (HAN) planners fall into two camps}~\cite{singamaneni2024survey}: learned planners capture complex behaviours but transfer poorly across robots and contexts, while model-based planners such as MPC transfer well but are context-blind and treat humans as obstacles with deterministic motion. My central hypothesis is that \textit{combining learned interaction and context models with stochastic model-based planning yields human-aware motion that is both adaptive and reliable}, a prerequisite for human-AI shared control. Building on my work in human-aware navigation, planning and HRI, I will address two research axes:

\vspace{0.5ex}
\noindent \textbf{\textit{Axis 1: Robust Human-Aware Motion Planning}}: How can a robot plan trajectories that remain safe, legible, and proactive under uncertainty in human motion, intention, and context, and transfer across platforms (mobile robots, cobots and robotic arms, humanoids)?

\vspace{0.5ex}
\noindent \textbf{\textit{Axis 2: Situation Assessment and Decision-Making in HRI}}: How should a robot assess an interaction situation, predict how humans will react to its actions, and select appropriate actions (e.g., move, wait, speak, hand over an object), including whether to lead, follow, or yield control to the human?

\vspace{0.5ex}
\boldheading{{Methodology}}

\noindent \textbf{{Robust and Adaptive Motion Planning (Axis 1)}}: Reinforcement learning (RL) and Imitation learning (IL) can learn controllers and behaviours that are hard to model mathematically, but usually need retraining on a new platform. Model Predictive Control (MPC)~\cite{kouvaritakis2016model} is flexible and transferable but requires precise modelling and tuning. My open-source co-planning MPC framework CoHAN~\ref{cohan1}, \ref{cohan2}, already adopted by other groups~\cite{sorokoletova2024enhancing}, plans joint human-robot trajectories with \textit{social constraints}, but assumes deterministic human motion. I will extend it to stochastic MPC (S-MPC)~\cite{heirung2018stochastic, zhu2025fusion}, where \textit{chance constraints}~\cite{paulson2020stochastic} encode human-interaction uncertainty as soft constraints and \textit{robust constraints} enforce kinematic feasibility and obstacle avoidance. RL will be used to learn the behaviours and cost terms that are hard to hand-design~\cite{jia2025architectures}, leaving safety to the S-MPC layer. Vision-language-action models (VLAs)~\cite{kawaharazuka2025vision} can interpret scenes and language to propose candidate actions, but in my experience generative models alone do not produce reliable trajectories without strong feedback. I will therefore use VLA outputs as guidance for the S-MPC, which ensures safety, responsiveness, and adherence to interaction constraints. Finally, to adapt to context, a learned context model (Axis 2) will update the constraints and objective of the S-MPC online, so that the same planner exhibits different behaviours in, e.g., a hospital corridor or a shared assembly cell. The same formulation applies to manipulators sharing a workspace with humans (e.g., handovers, collaborative assembly or waste sorting), where the joint plan covers both the robot's and the human's arm motions.

\vspace{0.5ex}
\noindent \textbf{{Learning Interaction Models and Decision-Making (Axis 2)}}: Recent HRI datasets such as \href{https://github.com/RobotiXX/MuSoHu-data-collection}{MuSoHu}, \href{https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~xiao/SCAND/SCAND.html}{SCAND}, \href{http://thor.oru.se/magni.html}{THÖR-Magni} and \href{https://jrdb.erc.monash.edu/dataset/social}{JRDB-Social}, including my own Bi$^3$ dataset~\ref{bi3dataset}, each cover few contexts and human densities, and carry collection biases (e.g., visible teleoperator vs autonomous robot). I will first address data harmonisation~\cite{loulaki2024addressing}: identifying common interaction features and combining datasets while accounting for their biases. On this data, I will learn a probabilistic action-reaction model that predicts how humans react (e.g., moving aside, waiting) to the robot's actions, using scene graphs~\cite{chang2021comprehensive} and graph neural networks (GNNs)~\cite{li2022dynamic} with attention~\cite{vaswani2017attention} to focus on relevant agents, and mixture density networks~\cite{errica2021graph} to output distributions that feed directly into the S-MPC chance constraints. For context recognition, I will fine-tune vision-language models (VLMs)~\cite{zhang2024vision} using LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora, luo2025towards} to classify the context from multimodal cues (vision, speech, gaze, body pose) and extract viable actions from the same scene graphs, and will move to dedicated architectures if this does not scale. In the mid-term, I will couple these predictions with decision-making under uncertainty (MDPs, POMDPs, RL)~\cite{rizk2018decision} and task planning~\cite{amiri2020learning}, extending my work on integrated task and motion planning~\ref{icsr24}, \ref{planrob} towards real-time, proactive decision-making for collaborative robots. This decision layer realises human-AI shared control: from the inferred human intent and context, it decides whether the robot leads, follows or yields, and how much autonomy it takes.

\vspace{0.5ex}
\noindent \textbf{{HRI Studies and Evaluation (Axes 1 and 2)}}: Both axes rely on understanding interaction. I will study how robots should communicate intent: when non-verbal cues such as motion legibility~\cite{schmidt2024investigating} suffice, and when verbal communication or negotiation is needed. LLMs can generate engaging speech~\cite{song2025goat} and multimodal LLMs can generate gestures~\cite{soton506590}, but their outputs are inconsistent across similar situations, so I will study how to constrain them for consistency and legibility. A series of user studies (following EU ethics guidelines) will identify the key features of interaction, whether agents' long-term intentions are needed or local observations suffice, and which constraints are common across contexts versus context-specific; these directly define the S-MPC constraints. In later years, I will extend this to physical HRI for whole-body human-aware motion planning. In parallel, I will continue my community work on evaluating HAN~\ref{principles}, \ref{new_metrics} by developing metrics, protocols, and benchmarks, notably through the \href{https://www.dagstuhl.de/seminars/seminar-calendar/seminar-details/27131}{Dagstuhl Seminar} I co-organise.

\noindent \textbf{{Obstacles and Mitigation}}: (i) \textit{Heterogeneous or insufficient data}: if harmonised public datasets are insufficient, I will collect targeted data on the team's platforms, building on my experience with the Bi$^3$ dual-site study~\ref{bi3dataset}. (ii) \textit{Unreliable generative models}: VLA/VLM outputs are only used as guidance; the S-MPC layer always enforces safety, so the system degrades gracefully to a classical planner. (iii) \textit{Sim-to-real and cross-platform transfer}: the model-based S-MPC layer reduces transfer to model identification, and every component is validated on real robots early through my open-source stack. (iv) \textit{Ethics and recruitment for user studies}: studies will follow institutional ethics review, and I will use simulation tools for human agents~\ref{inhus1}, \ref{imhus1} for early iterations.

\boldheading{{Integration in the Host Team}} This project fits the LIMELIGHT chair on \textit{Learning for Human-AI Shared Control} at LIRIS (UMR CNRS 5205). My co-planning approach, in which the robot plans jointly for itself and the human, is itself a form of human-AI shared control. In the IMAGINE team (main affiliation), it will provide the planning and control layer that links the team's expertise in learning, vision and multimodal perception (embodied AI, VLA) to safe, adaptive execution on real robots. With the SICAL team (secondary affiliation), I will collaborate on user studies, evaluation of interaction and intent communication, and interfaces for shared control. Axis~1 extends naturally from mobile robots to human-robot collaboration with manipulators, which I will validate on the LIRIS Robotics platform (cobots, robotic arms, industrial robots) in the chair's target applications, from assistive robots in healthcare to industrial assembly and waste sorting. The chair's ANR package (\texteuro 200k) will fund a first PhD student and experiments, and will seed ANR JCJC and ERC proposals. I will also bring my existing collaborations (LAAS-CNRS, INRIA, University of Michigan, euROBIN) to LIRIS.

\htwo{3.2.}{Teaching Project}
As I am actively involved in motion planning and control of autonomous social robotics, with the background of electronics, I plan to do teaching activities around  digital logic, embedded systems, robotics and motion planning, control, probabilistic robotics and localization, programming, decision-making and learning, and human-robot interaction. With growing usage of AI tools by students, my policy would be, to not completely restrict them from using them but to use them to understand or accelerate the work. For instance, I would limit their usage for first- and second-year students, while allowing more flexibility from third-year and Master’s students. I believe it is essential for students to first develop a strong foundation in coding and algorithm design independently before relying on AI to enhance their work.

At the level of Bachelors, I can teach digital logic, processor architectures, embedded systems and IoT (Arduino, AVR, Micro-controllers, communication protocols, etc.), hardware design, programming languages (C/C++/Python/Matlab) and data structures, signal processing and probability, introduction to robotics, etc. My teaching style would be a combination of slides with physical class room teaching and I am flexible to conduct online lectures if required. The course structure at the bachelors level would be lectures, assignments and a small project focused on the current state-of-the-art or applications. Depending on the course, I also plan to have labs involved in the courses I teach, which can either be used for the course project or dedicated experiments to get hands-on experience on the concepts taught in class. 

At the masters level, I will be focusing more on advanced topics like optimal motion planning and control, imitation and reinforcement learning, human-robot interaction, and evolving technologies for social robotics, AI and autonomous vehicles. I plan to make these courses more engaging by combining the teaching with in-class discussions on the current research papers. As social robotics is more practical, most courses will have projects as a part of course or the entire course could be a project on developing some methodologies. For the discussion sessions, students will be grouped and given papers that they need to read and understand before coming to the class. This works at the masters level as students are more autonomous.

My overall teaching activities aim to look at the real-world problems on social robotics and automation with focus on hands-on experience needed to build expert skills, critical thinking and deeper understanding of the concepts. I am flexible, adaptable, and always approachable when students need extra support or clarification in class.
